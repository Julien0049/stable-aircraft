Why does an aircraft's neutral point (NP) have to be located behind the center of gravity (CG) for stable flight?
AI

Longitudinal static stability requires that an uncommanded pitch disturbance (such as an upward vertical gust) naturally produces a corrective restoring pitching moment that pushes the nose back down.

dC_m / dα = C_mα < 0 (Criterion for Static Stability)
Static Margin (SM) = (x_NP - x_CG) / c_bar > 0

Key Physics:

  • Center of Gravity (CG): The pivot point where aircraft weight acts and around which pitching rotations occur.
  • Neutral Point (NP): The aerodynamic center of the whole aircraft, where additional lift ($\Delta L$) from an angle-of-attack change ($\Delta \alpha$) acts.
  • NP Behind CG (Stable): If a wind gust pushes the nose up ($\Delta \alpha > 0$), extra lift $\Delta L$ acts behind the pivot, pulling the tail up and forcing the nose back down.
  • NP In Front of CG (Unstable / Divergent): If the extra lift acts ahead of the pivot, it amplifies the pitch-up motion ($\Delta M > 0$), flipping the aircraft into a fatal stall.

💡 Pedagogical Value: Why This Interactive Explainer Surpasses Plain Text

1. Experiencing the Weathervane Lever Arm

Textbooks explain: "A positive static margin requires $x_{NP} > x_{CG}$." Here, you see the physical lever arm directly: when extra lift develops at the NP behind the pivot point, it acts just like the tail feathers of an arrow, naturally forcing the nose back into the relative wind.

2. Tangible Divergence & Runaway Stall

Drag the CG far into the tail (aft CG): when an upward gust strikes, the aircraft doesn't just fail to recover—the lift generates a positive feedback loop that pitches the nose higher and higher into a violent, unrecoverable departure.

3. Visualizing Modern Fly-By-Wire Tradeoffs

Why do fighter jets (like the F-16) fly with their NP slightly ahead of the CG? Try Lesson 3: neutral or slightly unstable aircraft turn with lightning agility and zero trim drag, but require high-speed computerized fly-by-wire computers to prevent aerodynamic disintegration.
